# Trip at a glance

| | |
|:--|:--|
| **Travellers** | 2 adults |
| **Dates** | Friday, May 14 to Saturday, May 22, 2027 (8 nights) |
| **Route** | Boston → Lisbon (4 nights) → Porto (4 nights) → Boston |
| **Pace** | Moderate: one main activity per day, afternoons partly free |
| **Estimated cost** | about **\$5,600** for two, including flights (see [Budget]) |

Mid-May is a good time to go: daytime highs are typically around 22–24 °C in
Lisbon and a few degrees cooler in Porto, the jacaranda trees in Lisbon start
to flower late in the month, and the summer crowds have not yet arrived.
Expect at least one day of rain in Porto.

# Day-by-day itinerary

## Day 1 · Friday, May 14 · Arrive in Lisbon

- **07:05** Land at Lisbon Airport (LIS) on the overnight flight from Boston.
- Take the **red metro line** from the airport to Saldanha and change to the
  yellow line for Rato, or take a taxi/ride-share (about €15–20, 25 minutes).
  Buy a rechargeable *Navegante* card at the station machine.
- **Hotel:** small boutique hotel in Príncipe Real. Check-in is 15:00; drop
  bags in the morning.
- Late morning: coffee and a *pastel de nata* at a local *pastelaria*, then
  an easy walk through Príncipe Real and down to the Miradouro de São Pedro de
  Alcântara for the first view over the city.
- Afternoon: nap. Seriously. Jet lag is worst on day one.
- **Evening:** early dinner in Bairro Alto or Chiado. Keep it light.

## Day 2 · Saturday, May 15 · Alfama and the castle

- **09:00** Ride **Tram 28** from Martim Moniz before the queues build. Get
  off at Largo das Portas do Sol.
- **10:00** Castelo de São Jorge (allow 1.5 hours; buy tickets online).
- Wander down through Alfama's lanes. Lunch: grilled sardines if they are in
  season, or *bitoque* at any small *tasca*.
- Afternoon: Lisbon Cathedral (Sé), then the National Tile Museum (Museu
  Nacional do Azulejo), a short taxi ride east. The museum is closed on
  Mondays.
- **20:30** **Fado dinner** in Alfama (reserve ahead; see [Reservations]).

## Day 3 · Sunday, May 16 · Belém

- **09:30** Tram 15E or a short train from Cais do Sodré to Belém.
- **10:00** Jerónimos Monastery. Book a timed ticket; the line without one
  can exceed an hour. Closed Mondays, which is why this is on a Sunday.
- **12:00** Pastéis de Belém for the original custard tarts (the queue for
  takeaway moves faster than the one for seats).
- Afternoon: walk along the river to the Padrão dos Descobrimentos and the
  Torre de Belém (photographs from outside are fine; the interior is small).
  MAAT (art, architecture and technology museum) if energy remains.
- **Evening:** sunset at the Miradouro da Senhora do Monte, dinner in Graça.

## Day 4 · Monday, May 17 · Day trip to Sintra

- **08:40** Train from **Rossio station** to Sintra (about 40 minutes, every
  20 minutes; covered by the Navegante card).
- **09:30** Bus 434 from Sintra station up to **Pena Palace**. Use the
  timed 09:30 or 10:00 entry slot booked in advance.
- Walk down through the Pena park to the **Moorish Castle** (optional, about
  1 hour) and on into the town.
- **Lunch** in Sintra town: try *travesseiros* at Piriquita.
- **15:00** **Quinta da Regaleira**, with its initiation well. Allow 2 hours.
- Train back to Lisbon around 18:00. Simple dinner near the hotel and pack.

> **Tip:** Sintra is cooler and often misty in the morning. Bring a layer
> and wear shoes with grip; the paths in the Pena park are steep.

## Day 5 · Tuesday, May 18 · Train to Porto

- **10:00** Check out. Taxi to **Lisboa Santa Apolónia**.
- **10:39** Alfa Pendular train to **Porto Campanhã** (about 3 hours;
  reserved seats in coach 2nd class). Buy food at the station or use the
  café car.
- **13:45** Connect from Campanhã to **São Bento** (one stop on any
  suburban train, about 5 minutes) and look up at the azulejo panels in the
  station hall on the way out.
- **Hotel:** in the Baixa/Aliados area, walking distance to everything.
- Late afternoon: walk down to the **Ribeira** waterfront and cross the
  lower deck of the **Dom Luís I bridge**.
- **Evening:** dinner on the Gaia side with a view of the Ribeira lights.

## Day 6 · Wednesday, May 19 · Old Porto and port wine

- **09:00** **Livraria Lello** at opening (timed ticket; the price is
  deductible from a book purchase).
- Clérigos Tower (climb 240 steps for the best view in the city), then
  Mercado do Bolhão for a snack.
- **Lunch:** a *francesinha*. It is enormous. Share one.
- **15:00** Walk across the **upper deck** of the Dom Luís I bridge to Vila
  Nova de Gaia. **Port cellar tour and tasting** (booked for 16:00).
- Take the Gaia cable car down to the river if your knees prefer it.
- Evening free.

## Day 7 · Thursday, May 20 · Douro Valley

- **08:00** Pick-up at the hotel for a **small-group Douro tour** (about
  10 hours): two quinta visits with tastings, lunch at a winery, and a one-hour
  boat trip on the river at Pinhão.
- Back in Porto around 18:30. Light dinner; you will have had plenty of wine.

## Day 8 · Friday, May 21 · Foz and a slow last day

- Morning: tram line 1 along the river to **Foz do Douro**, where the river
  meets the Atlantic. Walk the seafront promenade to the Pérgola da Foz.
- **Lunch:** fresh fish in Matosinhos (taxi 10 minutes from Foz).
- Afternoon: Serralves Museum and park, or simply back to the hotel to rest
  and pack.
- **Evening:** farewell dinner. Book something a bit special.

## Day 9 · Saturday, May 22 · Fly home

- **08:00** Metro line E (violet) from Trindade to Porto Airport (OPO),
  about 30 minutes, or taxi (€25–30).
- **11:10** Flight Porto → Boston.

Per person, that comes to about **\$2,800**. The biggest levers if the total
needs to come down:

1. Choose the Douro by train instead of a guided tour (the scenic Douro line
   from São Bento to Pinhão costs a fraction as much, but you lose the winery
   visits and need to plan lunch).
2. Stay in a guesthouse instead of a hotel in Porto (saves roughly €40 a
   night).
3. Swap the farewell dinner for a picnic of market food at a viewpoint.

# Practical notes

**Money.** Cards are accepted almost everywhere, but carry €40–60 in cash
for small cafés, markets and tips. Decline "dynamic currency conversion" at
card terminals and pay in euros.

**Tipping.** Not expected as in the US. Rounding up or leaving 5–10% for good
table service is generous.

**Getting around.** Lisbon and Porto are both very hilly, with slippery
limestone pavements (*calçada*). Comfortable shoes with good grip matter more
than anything else you pack.

**Phones.** Install an eSIM before departure. Ride-share apps work in both
cities.

**Emergency.** The European emergency number is **112**. Save the address and
phone number of both hotels offline, along with the US Embassy in Lisbon.

**Opening days.** Many museums close on Mondays. The plan already accounts
for this (Belém on Sunday, Sintra on Monday), so keep it in mind if days
are swapped around.
