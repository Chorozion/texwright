# Soups for Cold Evenings {#ch:soups}

There is a particular kind of quiet that settles over a kitchen when a pot of
soup has been left to its own devices. The windows fog, the house smells of
onions and bay, and everyone who comes through the door asks the same
question: *what are we having?*

Soup was the first thing I learned to cook without a recipe, standing on a
chair beside my grandmother, who measured salt in her palm and never once
used a timer. This chapter is my attempt to write down what she knew by
feel. The recipes start with a stock, because a good stock is the difference
between soup and flavoured water, and then move from the simplest to the
most involved.

A few things apply to every recipe here:

- **Season at the end.** Stock reduces as it simmers, and soup that tasted
  right at the start will be too salty after an hour. Add most of the salt in
  the last ten minutes.
- **Acid wakes everything up.** If a soup tastes flat and more salt doesn't
  help, it almost always needs a squeeze of lemon or a splash of vinegar.
- **Soup is better the next day.** Nearly all of these can be made ahead and
  reheated gently. The exceptions are noted.

## Golden Chicken Stock

*Makes about 3 litres (3 quarts) · Active time 20 minutes · Total time 4 hours*

I make this on Sunday afternoons and freeze it in 500 ml tubs. Roasting the
bones first gives a deeper colour and a rounder flavour; if you are short of
time you can skip it, and the stock will simply be lighter.

**Ingredients**

- 2 kg (4½ lb) chicken wings, backs or carcasses
- 2 onions, halved, skins left on
- 2 carrots, cut in large chunks
- 2 celery sticks, cut in large chunks
- 1 head garlic, halved crosswise
- 1 small bunch parsley stems
- 2 bay leaves
- 10 black peppercorns
- 4 litres (4 quarts) cold water

**Method**

1. Heat the oven to 220 °C (425 °F). Spread the chicken, onions and carrots
   on a large roasting tray and roast for 40–45 minutes, turning once, until
   deeply browned.
2. Transfer everything to a large stockpot. Pour a cup of water into the hot
   tray and scrape up the browned bits; add this to the pot.
3. Add the celery, garlic, parsley, bay and peppercorns, then the cold
   water. Bring slowly to a bare simmer over medium heat, skimming off the
   grey foam as it rises.
4. Simmer, uncovered, for 3 hours. Do not let it boil hard, or the stock
   will turn cloudy and greasy.
5. Strain through a fine sieve, pressing lightly on the solids. Cool quickly
   (a sink of iced water works well), then refrigerate overnight and lift off
   the fat.

> **Keeps:** 4 days in the fridge, 6 months in the freezer.

## Tuscan White Bean and Kale Soup

*Serves 6 · Active time 25 minutes · Total time 1 hour*

This is a weeknight cousin of *ribollita*, the Tuscan bread soup. Blending a
cup of the beans and stirring them back in makes it creamy without any
cream. The parmesan rind is not essential but it adds a savoury depth that
is hard to get any other way, so start saving them in a bag in the freezer.

**Ingredients**

- 3 tbsp olive oil, plus more to serve
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 2 carrots, diced
- 2 celery sticks, diced
- 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1 tsp chopped fresh rosemary (or ½ tsp dried)
- ¼ tsp chilli flakes
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 2 × 400 g (14 oz) tins cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
- 1.5 litres (6 cups) chicken or vegetable stock
- 1 parmesan rind (optional)
- 1 bunch (about 250 g) cavolo nero or curly kale, stems removed, leaves
  torn
- Salt and black pepper
- 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
- Grated parmesan and crusty bread, to serve

**Method**

1. Warm the oil in a large, heavy pot over medium heat. Add the onion,
   carrots and celery with a pinch of salt and cook, stirring now and then,
   until soft and just starting to colour, about 10 minutes.
2. Add the garlic, rosemary and chilli flakes and cook for 1 minute, until
   fragrant. Stir in the tomato paste and cook for 2 minutes more, until it
   darkens slightly.
3. Add the beans, stock and parmesan rind. Bring to a boil, then reduce the
   heat and simmer for 20 minutes.
4. Ladle about 1 cup of beans and a little liquid into a blender (or use a
   stick blender in a jug), purée until smooth, and stir back into the pot.
5. Add the kale and simmer for 8–10 minutes, until tender. Remove the
   parmesan rind.
6. Season with salt, pepper and the vinegar. Serve with a generous drizzle
   of olive oil, grated parmesan and bread.

> **Variation:** For a heartier soup, brown 250 g (½ lb) crumbled Italian
> sausage before adding the vegetables, and reduce the olive oil to 1 tbsp.

## Red Lentil Soup with Coconut and Lime

*Serves 4 · Active time 15 minutes · Total time 40 minutes*

Red lentils collapse as they cook, so this soup thickens itself with no
blending required. It is my standby when the fridge is nearly empty: the
pantry almost always has lentils, a tin of coconut milk and a jar of curry
paste.

**Ingredients**

- 2 tbsp neutral oil or coconut oil
- 1 onion, chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, grated
- 1 thumb-sized piece ginger (about 20 g), grated
- 2–3 tbsp Thai red curry paste[^paste]
- 1 tsp ground turmeric
- 250 g (1¼ cups) red lentils, rinsed until the water runs clear
- 1 litre (4 cups) vegetable stock or water
- 1 × 400 ml (14 fl oz) tin coconut milk
- 1 tbsp fish sauce or soy sauce
- Juice of 1–2 limes
- Handful of spinach (optional)
- To serve: chopped coriander (cilantro), sliced spring onions, chilli oil

[^paste]: Brands vary enormously in heat and saltiness. Start with 2 tbsp,
    taste at the end of step 3, and stir in more if you like.

**Method**

1. Heat the oil in a pot over medium heat and cook the onion for 5 minutes,
   until soft.
2. Add the garlic, ginger, curry paste and turmeric and fry, stirring
   constantly, for 2 minutes. The paste should smell toasty and start to
   stick slightly.
3. Add the lentils, stock and all but 4 tablespoons of the coconut milk.
   Bring to a boil, then simmer, partly covered, for 20–25 minutes, stirring
   occasionally, until the lentils have broken down completely.
4. Stir in the fish sauce, the juice of one lime and the spinach, if using.
   Taste and add more lime or salt as needed. If it is too thick, loosen it
   with a splash of water.
5. Serve in warm bowls, swirled with the reserved coconut milk and scattered
   with coriander, spring onions and a few drops of chilli oil.

> **Make ahead:** The soup thickens considerably in the fridge. Thin it with
> water or stock when reheating, and add the lime juice fresh.

## French Onion Soup

*Serves 4 · Active time 30 minutes · Total time 2 hours*

The only secret to this soup is patience. The onions need a full hour to turn
from sharp and white to soft, sweet and the colour of strong tea. Turning up
the heat to speed things along will scorch them and make the soup bitter.

**Ingredients**

- 50 g (3½ tbsp) butter
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1.5 kg (3¼ lb) yellow onions, halved and thinly sliced
- 1 tsp salt
- ½ tsp sugar (only if your onions are not browning)
- 3 sprigs thyme
- 2 tbsp plain (all-purpose) flour
- 150 ml (⅔ cup) dry white wine or dry sherry
- 1.5 litres (6 cups) beef stock, or half beef and half chicken
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 tbsp brandy or cognac (optional)
- 8 slices baguette, about 2 cm (¾ in) thick, toasted
- 200 g (7 oz) Gruyère, coarsely grated

**Method**

1. Melt the butter with the oil in a wide, heavy pot over medium-low heat.
   Add the onions and salt and stir to coat. Cover and cook for 15 minutes,
   until the onions have released their liquid and collapsed.
2. Uncover and continue cooking, stirring every 5 minutes or so and scraping
   the bottom of the pot, for 45–60 minutes, until the onions are a deep
   golden brown. If they start to catch, add a splash of water and scrape.
3. Stir in the thyme and flour and cook for 2 minutes. Add the wine and let
   it bubble until almost evaporated.
4. Add the stock and bay leaf. Simmer gently for 30 minutes. Remove the
   thyme stalks and bay, stir in the brandy, and season to taste.
5. Heat the grill (broiler). Ladle the soup into heatproof bowls set on a
   baking tray. Float two slices of toast on each, cover thickly with
   Gruyère and grill for 3–5 minutes, until bubbling and browned in spots.

> **A note on bowls:** Ordinary bowls can crack under a grill. If you don't
> have ovenproof ones, grill the cheese on the toast separately on a tray and
> float the finished toasts on the soup.

## Measurements and substitutions

All spoon measurements are level. A tablespoon is 15 ml and a teaspoon is
5 ml. Cup measurements are US cups (240 ml).

| Ingredient       | 1 cup       | Notes                                   |
|:-----------------|:------------|:----------------------------------------|
| Water or stock   | 240 ml      |                                         |
| Red lentils      | 200 g       | Rinse before measuring                  |
| Grated Gruyère   | 100 g       | Loosely packed                          |
| Plain flour      | 125 g       | Spooned and levelled                    |
| Sliced onions    | 115 g       | Raw, thinly sliced                      |

| Oven setting     | °C   | °F   | Gas mark |
|:-----------------|-----:|-----:|---------:|
| Moderate         | 180  | 350  | 4        |
| Moderately hot   | 200  | 400  | 6        |
| Hot              | 220  | 425  | 7        |

If you are cooking for someone who is vegetarian, every soup in this chapter
works with vegetable stock, except the French onion soup, which loses a great
deal without beef stock. A strong mushroom stock made with dried porcini is
the best substitute I have found.
