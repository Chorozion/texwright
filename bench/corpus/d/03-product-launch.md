**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

**AMES, Iowa — October 7, 2026** — Tallgrass Instruments, Inc. today
announced Rootline S2, a solar-free wireless soil probe that measures
moisture, temperature and electrical conductivity at six depths down to 60 cm
and reports readings to growers' phones every 15 minutes. Rootline S2 is
available to order today at a launch price of \$289 per probe, with shipping
to customers in the United States and Canada beginning November 3.

Most farms that irrigate still decide when to water by walking fields,
pushing a soil probe in by hand, or following a fixed calendar. Commercial
moisture-monitoring systems exist, but they are typically priced and
supported for large operations: a single station often costs more than
\$1,500 installed and carries an annual data fee. Rootline S2 was designed
for the operations in between: vegetable growers on 20 to 400 acres,
orchards, vineyards, and university extension plots.

"We kept hearing the same thing from growers: 'I know I'm overwatering some
blocks and underwatering others, but I can't afford to put a sensor in every
one,'" said Maya Lindqvist, co-founder and chief executive officer of
Tallgrass Instruments. "Rootline S2 is cheap enough to put one in every
management zone. When you can see water move down through the root zone
after an irrigation set, you stop guessing."

## What's new in Rootline S2

Rootline S2 replaces the original Rootline probe, which shipped to about
2,300 customers in 2024 and 2025. The new model was redesigned around
feedback from those growers and from three seasons of field trials with
partner farms in Iowa, California's Central Valley and Ontario.

- **Six sensing depths.** Capacitive sensors at 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 and
  60 cm measure volumetric water content, so growers can see whether
  irrigation is reaching the root zone or draining past it. The original
  Rootline measured at three depths.
- **Electrical conductivity at every depth.** Salinity readings help
  growers using fertigation or reclaimed water spot salt build-up before it
  affects yield.
- **Five-year battery.** A replaceable lithium thionyl chloride cell powers
  the probe for an expected five years at the default 15-minute reporting
  interval.[^battery] There is no solar panel to clean, shade or steal.
- **Long-range radio.** Probes report over LoRaWAN to a Rootline Gateway at
  up to 5 km line of sight, or to any compatible third-party LoRaWAN
  network. One gateway supports up to 200 probes.
- **Install in under ten minutes.** A tapered polycarbonate body goes into a
  pilot hole made with the included auger. No slurry and no digging.
- **No subscription.** The Rootline app for iOS, Android and the web is free
  for up to 25 probes. Data can be exported to CSV at any time or streamed
  to other farm-management software through an open REST API.

[^battery]: Battery life estimated at 20 °C average soil temperature with
    readings every 15 minutes and a gateway within 2 km. Colder conditions,
    shorter reporting intervals and longer radio links reduce battery life.

## Built with growers

During the 2025 and 2026 seasons, 41 partner farms ran Rootline S2
prototypes alongside their existing practice. At Hollis Family Farm, a
140-acre mixed-vegetable operation near Grinnell, Iowa, the probes showed
that drip sets on the farm's sandy north field were pushing water below
50 cm within 90 minutes.

"We cut our sets on that field from three hours to an hour and forty-five
minutes, and the plants did better, not worse," said Tom Hollis, who manages
irrigation on the farm. "Over the season we pumped about 30 percent less
water there. The probes paid for themselves in electricity before July."

Results vary with soil, crop and climate, and Tallgrass does not promise
specific water savings. The company has published the partner-farm data,
with growers' permission, in an anonymised report on its website.
