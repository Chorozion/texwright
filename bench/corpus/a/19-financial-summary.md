## Highlights

- **Revenue rose 13.7% year over year to $450,300**, driven by wholesale (+125%) and retail bean sales (+23%). Café beverage sales grew 9.4%.
- **Net income was $75,750, up 27.4%** from $59,440 in Q3 2025. Net margin improved from 15.0% to 16.8%.
- **Gross margin slipped one point, to 69.0%**, as green coffee costs rose and wholesale, which carries lower margins, grew as a share of sales.
- **Labor as a share of revenue fell to 31.4%** from 32.4%, the result of the new scheduling system introduced in June.
- **Cash ended the quarter at $114,600**, up $18,200, after an $18,500 espresso machine purchase and a $45,000 owner distribution.

All figures are unaudited and prepared on an accrual basis. Harbor Street is an S corporation; income taxes are paid by the owners and are not shown in these statements.

### Revenue

Wholesale was the standout. We added two restaurant accounts in May (Saltmarsh Kitchen and The Larder), which together bought $11,900 of beans this quarter. Wholesale is now 6.2% of revenue, up from 3.1% a year ago. Retail bean sales grew on the strength of the subscription program, which reached 214 active subscribers at quarter end, compared with 131 a year ago.

Café revenue growth came from both traffic and price. Transactions rose 5.6%, and the average café ticket increased from $7.09 to $7.30 (+3.0%), reflecting the $0.25 price increase on espresso drinks in April.

### Cost of goods sold

COGS rose 17.4%, faster than revenue. Two factors explain most of the increase:

1. **Green coffee prices.** Our average cost per pound of green coffee was $6.85, compared with $5.90 in Q3 2025 (+16%). We roast about 7,400 lb per quarter, so this alone added roughly $7,000 to COGS.
2. **Sales mix.** Wholesale beans carry a gross margin of about 48%, compared with about 72% for café beverages. As wholesale grows, the blended margin falls even if each product line's margin holds steady.

### Operating expenses

Operating expenses grew 8.1%, well below revenue growth, so the operating margin widened from 15.7% to 17.3%. Wages rose 9.9% because of the July minimum wage increase and one additional part-time barista, but they fell as a share of revenue. Repairs dropped by $2,450 after we replaced the old espresso machine, which had needed three service calls in Q3 2025.

## Outlook for Q4

Q4 is usually our strongest quarter, helped by holiday gift sales of beans and merchandise. For Q4 2026 we expect:

- **Revenue of $485,000 to $505,000**, up 9–13% from $446,200 in Q4 2025, including about $34,000 of wholesale revenue now that all three accounts are at full volume.
- **Gross margin of 68–69%.** Our green coffee contract for October–December is locked at $6.95 per pound, so further cost increases are unlikely this year.
- **One-time costs of about $6,500** for a point-of-sale system upgrade in November.

We recommend keeping the Q4 owner distribution at $45,000 and building cash toward the $150,000 reserve target agreed in January, which would cover about two months of operating expenses.
