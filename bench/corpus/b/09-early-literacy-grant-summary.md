## 1. Summary

Too many children in Marlow County start kindergarten without the early language and literacy skills they need. *Read Early, Read Together* will reach 320 four-year-olds in the year before kindergarten in the county's four lowest-income attendance zones and give their families the books, coaching and routines that predict reading success. The program combines monthly home visits by bilingual family literacy coaches, a book of the child's own every month, quarterly family reading nights at branch libraries, and coaching for the preschool teachers who serve the same children. We expect participating children to enter kindergarten meeting early literacy benchmarks at a rate at least 14 percentage points higher than the current rate in these zones.

## 2. Statement of need

Reading proficiency by the end of third grade is one of the strongest predictors of later school success. Students who are not reading proficiently by then are about four times more likely to leave high school without a diploma than proficient readers (Hernandez, 2011). Nationally, only 31% of fourth graders scored at or above the Proficient level on the 2024 National Assessment of Educational Progress in reading (NCES, 2024).

The gap opens before school starts. In fall 2025:

- **41%** of Marlow County kindergartners met the composite benchmark on the DIBELS 8th Edition beginning-of-year screening, compared with **56%** statewide.
- In the four target attendance zones, the rate was **29%**. These zones also have the county's highest rates of child poverty (34%) and of households where a language other than English is spoken at home (22%).
- Only **38%** of children in the target zones attended a formal preschool program, and the nearest library branch is more than 3 miles away for half of these families.

A 2025 survey of 212 caregivers at Tri-County Head Start found that 46% read with their child at least five days a week, and 27% reported having fewer than ten children's books at home. Families consistently told us that they want to read with their children but lack books, time, and confidence, especially when they are not confident readers themselves or are more comfortable in Spanish.

## 3. Evidence base

The program design rests on three well-established findings.

1. **Shared reading builds language.** Dialogic reading, in which the adult asks open-ended questions and expands on the child's answers, produces larger gains in expressive vocabulary than ordinary reading aloud (Whitehurst et al., 1988). A meta-analysis of 16 studies found a moderate effect of dialogic reading on oral language, strongest for children ages 2 to 3 (Mol et al., 2008).
2. **Early code-related skills predict later reading.** Alphabet knowledge, phonological awareness, and rapid naming measured before kindergarten predict later decoding, spelling and comprehension (National Early Literacy Panel, 2008).
3. **Book access plus guidance changes home routines.** Programs that give families books together with guidance from a trusted professional increase the frequency of reading at home and improve children's receptive language (Zuckerman, 2009).

## 4. Goals and objectives

**Goal:** Increase kindergarten readiness in early literacy for children in Marlow County's four highest-need attendance zones.

## 5. Program design

**Monthly home visits.** Two half-time family literacy coaches, at least one bilingual in Spanish, will visit each family for about 45 minutes once a month for ten months, from September or October through June. Younger siblings are welcome at every visit. Each visit models dialogic reading with that month's book, introduces one early literacy activity (rhyming, letter play, storytelling), and sets a simple reading goal for the month. Families who prefer may meet at a library branch or by video.

**A book every month.** Each child receives a new, age-appropriate book at every visit, plus two books at enrollment, for a total of 12 per program year. Books are available in English, Spanish, and bilingual editions and are selected with a children's librarian to reflect the families served.

**Family reading nights.** Four evenings per year at the Eastside and Riverbend branches, with dinner, child care for younger siblings, a storytime, and a short workshop for caregivers. Every family receives a library card at the first event.

**Teacher coaching.** The district's early learning coach will lead three workshops per year on dialogic reading and phonological awareness for 24 preschool and Head Start teachers in the target zones, followed by two classroom observations with feedback. Teachers receive a stipend of $400 for full participation in Year 1 and $200 for a lighter refresher sequence in Year 2.

**Referral partnership.** Marlow Family Health Clinic will refer families at well-child visits for children ages 2 to 4, giving the program a direct enrollment path for families not connected to preschool.

## 6. Evaluation plan

An external evaluator, the Center for Applied Education Research at Hollins State University, will conduct the evaluation. Data collection is summarized below.

| Instrument | Who | When | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enrollment and attendance records | Coaches | Ongoing | Dosage; objective 4 |
| PPVT-5 | Evaluator | Enrollment and end of program year | Objective 2 |
| Family literacy survey (English/Spanish) | Coaches | Enrollment, midyear, end of year | Objective 3 |
| DIBELS 8 beginning-of-year | District | Fall of kindergarten | Objective 1 |
| Classroom observation checklist | Early learning coach | Twice per year | Objective 5 |
| Caregiver focus groups | Evaluator | Spring of each year | Program improvement |

To estimate the program's effect on kindergarten readiness, the evaluator will compare participants with children from the same attendance zones who entered kindergarten in fall 2025 and fall 2026, adjusting for age, home language, and preschool attendance. Interim results will be shared with the Foundation in January 2029. The final report will follow Cohort 2's fall 2029 kindergarten screening and be delivered by December 2029.

The library foundation will contribute in-kind support valued at about $46,000 over two years, including meeting space, a children's librarian's time for book selection, and program marketing. Marlow County Schools will contribute the early learning coach's time for teacher coaching (about $18,000). The cost per child served is about $1,294, or roughly $129 for each month of home-visiting service.

## 9. Sustainability

The program is designed to outlast the grant. Book costs will shift in Year 3 to the county's Dolly Parton's Imagination Library affiliate, which the library foundation has agreed to expand to all children under five in the target zones. The library board has approved a request in the fiscal year 2030 county budget to retain one family literacy coach position, contingent on the interim evaluation. Teacher coaching will continue through the district's professional development calendar, and the trained teachers themselves remain in the classrooms.

## 10. Organizational capacity

The Marlow County Public Library has run early literacy programming since 1998, including weekly bilingual storytimes at all five branches that reached 9,400 attendees in 2025. The library foundation has managed 14 restricted grants over the past decade, including a $250,000 state digital access grant completed on time and on budget in 2024. Tri-County Head Start serves 410 children in the county and has a family engagement team with long experience in home visiting.
